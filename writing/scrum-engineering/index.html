Engineering organisations as systems

The Scrum–Engineering Model

Actors → Tools → Process → Storm

An org drowning in on-call is an org in an interrupt storm. A team planning execution nine months ahead is a team whose working set doesn’t fit in cache. Both are scheduling failures. Both come out of the org budget.

A system consistently averaging 90%+90\%+ utilisation is a system with little headroom and rapidly rising latency. Engineering organisations invariably get their prioritisation wrong and ship ad hoc — get it out first and fix it later1 — in the name of a dynamic2 work culture.

Capacity that is somehow both fully utilised and permanently behind is a conundrum faced by organisations large and small. “work harder™” doesn’t make this better. It makes it worse. If utilisation is consistently ≥100%≥ 100\%, bad things may happen™.

Computer science has spent fifty years on versions of this problem, and the fundamentals transfer. Organisations and leaders can either just work harder™ and wither, or apply the basics and weather this storm. Where I did it, on-call volume went from roughly six thousand issues a year to roughly six hundred. But the tools and the measurements3 have to exist first4, which is where this starts.

The parts

  • Part I The Actors Who — and what is each good for? Taxonomy, and the two dogmas.
  • Part II The Tools With what — where does the queue actually live? The JIRA rehabilitation.
  • Part III The Process How — ticks, batch size, bounded lookahead. Nine months of plan delivered in three.
  • Part IV The Storm Interrupts — two queues, top and bottom halves. Six thousand on-calls a year into six hundred.
